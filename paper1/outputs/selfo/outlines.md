# Outlines — selfo

**Thesis (fixed):** The asymmetry between self- and other-ascription is real but inverted: others have superior interpretive access to our standing attitudes (functional-dispositional patterns) because they observe behavioral patterns without motivated reasoning, while we have privileged non-interpretive access only to occurrent judgments in the moment of avowal and to the evidence (memory traces, private feelings) we use for self-interpretation

Nothing here is ranked and nothing is scored. The outlines were written blind to each other, and the flags below each one are computed from its own dependency ledger — no model judged coherence. Read the outlines first; the divergence section at the end is only useful once you have.

## Outline 0 — The Inverted Asymmetry: Why Others Know Your Mind Better Than You Do

*Establish that standing attitudes are behavioral-dispositional patterns requiring interpretive access, show that self-interpretation suffers systematic motivated reasoning distortions while other-interpretation does not, then argue that privileged first-person access applies only to occurrent states and self-interpretive evidence rather than to the standing attitudes themselves.*

- **Surprise.** The argument grants more to first-person privilege than usual skeptical positions (acknowledging genuine non-interpretive access to occurrent states and evidence) while still reaching a strongly revisionary conclusion. The structure places the scope-narrowing move before rather than after the motivated reasoning argument, making the case that we never had the kind of access that would matter.
- **Commitment.** The paper commits to an empirical claim that motivated reasoning is sufficiently systematic and ineliminable to override evidential advantages, rather than treating it as one correctable bias among others. It also commits to functionalism about standing attitudes strongly enough that no amount of introspective phenomenology could constitute direct knowledge of them.
- **Gap.** §4, inductive: The empirical evidence from social psychology about motivated reasoning in self-relevant contexts would have to generalize reliably to the philosophical domain of standing attitude interpretation, and the magnitude of distortion would have to be sufficient to outweigh any first-person evidential advantages systematically rather than occasionally.
- **Arrives at.** §7
- **Load-bearing.** Knowledge of what dispositional pattern one instantiates requires interpretation of behavioral evidence across contexts rather than direct inspection of any single state.

## Outline 1 — The Inverted Asymmetry: Why Others Know Our Minds Better Than We Do

*Distinguish occurrent from standing states, show traditional privileged access applies only to the former, then argue systematic biases make us worse interpreters of our own standing attitudes than external observers are.*

- **Surprise.** The argument inverts rather than dissolves the asymmetry, and does so by beginning with metaphysics of mental states rather than with epistemology. Most contemporary work either defends traditional first-person authority or argues for symmetrical unreliability; a systematic other-superiority claim for a major class of mental states has no clear precedent in recent literature.
- **Commitment.** The paper commits to a genuinely bifurcated picture of self-knowledge where first-person authority is robust in one domain and systematically inferior in another, rather than hedging with 'sometimes' or 'to some degree'. It also commits to the empirical claim that motivated reasoning distorts self-interpretation of standing attitudes reliably enough to ground a philosophical thesis, not merely creates statistical tendencies.
- **Gap.** §5, abductive: The absence of motivated reasoning and the presence of more complete behavioral data must be epistemically more valuable than privileged access to private evidence and reasoning processes when the interpretive task is identifying functional-dispositional patterns—this requires that pattern-detection advantages outweigh information advantages decisively enough to overcome the intuition that more evidence makes better interpretation.
- **Arrives at.** §7
- **Load-bearing.** Attributing standing attitudes to anyone, including oneself, necessarily requires interpreting behavioral patterns across contexts because standing attitudes are individuated by their functional-dispositional profiles.

## Outline 2 — Against First-Personal Authority: The Interpretive Superiority of Others for Standing Attitudes

*Establish that standing attitudes are functional-dispositional patterns requiring interpretation, demonstrate that self-interpretation suffers from systematic motivated distortions, and conclude that others' observational access yields superior interpretation despite subjects retaining non-interpretive access only to occurrent states.*

- **Surprise.** The outline treats occurrent access not as an objection to be answered but as a structural component of the thesis itself, using the delimitation of genuine first-personal authority to sharpen rather than threaten the inversion claim. Most arguments for other-knowledge superiority must defensively explain away first-person authority; this one incorporates it.
- **Commitment.** The argument commits to the empirical claim that motivated reasoning distortions are not merely occasional or correctable but systematic and ineliminable in self-interpretation, making this a matter of structural epistemic position rather than contingent psychological failing. A cautious version would claim only that others sometimes have equal or better access in specific cases.
- **Gap.** §5, abductive: For this inference to succeed, it must be true that the magnitude of motivated reasoning distortion in self-interpretation exceeds the evidential disadvantage others face from lacking access to private phenomenology and memory, such that the net interpretive accuracy favors external observation despite its limitations.
- **Arrives at.** §7
- **Load-bearing.** Self-interpretation of standing attitudes is systematically distorted by motivated reasoning in ways that favor self-conceptions over accuracy.

## Outline 3 — The Inverted Asymmetry: Why Others Know Our Minds Better Than We Do

*Distinguish types of mental states and access modes, then show that observational access to behavioral patterns systematically outperforms introspective access to functional-dispositional states while conceding introspective privilege only for occurrent judgments and private evidence.*

- **Surprise.** The paper defends other-superiority for standing attitudes while conceding traditional first-person authority for occurrent states, bifurcating the mental in a way the traditional debate does not. Most revisionist views either deny the asymmetry entirely (Gopnik, Carruthers) or preserve it for all mental states (Burge, Moran); this outline claims others know our minds better precisely where it matters most for character and identity.
- **Commitment.** The outline commits to an empirical claim about the relative magnitude of two epistemic defects—motivated reasoning in self-interpretation versus evidential limitation in other-interpretation—that cautious versions could leave as 'depends on circumstances' or 'varies by person.' It also commits to functionalism about standing attitudes without hedging into a weaker 'if functionalism, then...' conditional, making the thesis hostage to that metaphysics.
- **Gap.** §5, inductive: The advantage observers gain from impartiality in pattern recognition must outweigh whatever advantage subjects gain from access to private evidence, and this must hold generally across persons and attitude types rather than merely in some cases—this requires that motivated reasoning be powerful enough and pervasive enough to systematically corrupt self-interpretation despite subjects having more total evidence.
- **Arrives at.** §7
- **Load-bearing.** Third-person observers systematically outperform first-person subjects at detecting behavioral patterns across time and context because observers aggregate data without filtering through motivated self-narrative.

## Outline 4 — The Inverted Asymmetry: Why Others Know Our Minds Better Than We Do

*Establish that standing attitudes require interpretation, show that self-interpretation is systematically biased while other-interpretation is not, then demonstrate that only occurrent states and interpretive evidence remain privileged to the self.*

- **Surprise.** The paper treats other-superiority as the default requiring no special justification and makes first-person authority the phenomenon that needs explaining and limiting. - **Load-bearing.** Accessing what standing attitudes a person has requires interpreting behavioral patterns rather than simple observation, for both self and other cases.

## Outline 5 — Against First-Personal Authority: The Interpretive Superiority of Others for Standing Attitudes

*Establish what privileged access actually covers (occurrent states and private evidence), then show why third-personal observation systematically outperforms self-interpretation for standing attitudes, then argue this produces genuine interpretive asymmetry favoring others.*

- **Surprise.** The structure front-loads the positive case for third-personal superiority before delimiting first-personal access, inverting the standard dialectic where residual self-authority is established first and then qualified. This makes the paper's burden defending an unusual existential claim (others are better) rather than a familiar negative one (selves aren't as good as thought).
- **Commitment.** The outline commits to a clean metaphysical division where standing attitudes are purely functional-dispositional with no irreducible phenomenal or narrative component accessible only first-personally; a cautious version would allow that standing attitudes have both dispositional and phenomenal-narrative aspects, claiming other-superiority only for the former.
- **Gap.** §5, abductive: The observational and motivational advantages documented must be sufficient to overcome any compensating epistemic advantages available to self-interpreters (such as greater sample size of observations, access to counterfactual reasoning about unrealized dispositions, or integration of private evidence) such that net interpretive position favors others.
- **Arrives at.** §8
- **Load-bearing.** Others have superior interpretive access to our standing attitudes because they observe behavioral patterns necessary for knowing functional-dispositional structures without the motivated reasoning that distorts self-interpretation.

## Divergence

**Forced** — every outline does this, so the thesis requires it:

- Distinguish standing attitudes (functional-dispositional patterns) from occurrent judgments or states
- Establish that knowing standing attitudes requires interpreting behavioral patterns across contexts rather than direct inspection
- Argue that self-interpretation of standing attitudes suffers from motivated reasoning distortions
- Argue that others observe behavioral patterns without the motivated reasoning that distorts self-interpretation
- Conclude that others have superior interpretive access to standing attitudes
- Establish that first-person privilege is restricted to occurrent judgments in the moment of avowal
- Establish that first-person privilege extends to private evidence (memory traces, feelings) used for self-interpretation
- Conclude with the thesis statement that the asymmetry is real but inverted

**Free** — the outlines split, so this is yours to choose:

- **whether to establish the nature of standing attitudes before or after discussing access modes** — establish what standing attitudes are (functional-dispositional patterns) before discussing any access claims; interleave the nature of standing attitudes with claims about access modes *(all outlines establish standing attitudes first in section 1)*
- **when to introduce first-person privilege and its scope** — establish first-person privilege early (section 2-3) before arguing for third-person superiority; establish third-person superiority first, then restrict first-person privilege later (section 5-6) *(outlines 1, 3, 4 establish first-person privilege early; outlines 0, 2, 5 establish third-person superiority first)*
- **whether to treat occurrent judgments and private evidence together or separately** — treat occurrent judgments and private evidence as two aspects of first-person privilege in a single section; dedicate separate sections to occurrent judgments and to private evidence *(outlines 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 treat them together; outline 5 separates them (sections 6 and 7))*
- **whether to address the social authority or appearance of self-knowledge** — include a section explaining why avowals carry social authority despite being interpretively unreliable; omit discussion of social authority and focus only on epistemic reliability *(only outline 3 includes this (section 6); all others omit it)*
- **whether to explicitly argue that self-ascription is interpretive inference from privileged evidence** — include a dedicated section establishing that self-ascription of standing attitudes is interpretive inference from privileged evidence rather than direct awareness; treat this point as implicit in the distinction between evidence and interpretation *(outlines 2, 3, 4 make this explicit in dedicated sections; outlines 0, 1, 5 treat it implicitly)*
- **the order of establishing motivated reasoning versus observational advantages** — establish motivated reasoning distortions in self-interpretation before discussing others' observational advantages; establish others' observational advantages before or simultaneously with motivated reasoning claims *(outlines 0, 1, 2, 4 establish motivated reasoning first; outline 3 establishes observational advantages first (section 2); outline 5 treats them together)*
- **whether memory traces and private feelings constitute an evidential advantage that is ultimately overcome** — explicitly argue that private evidence provides an advantage that is nonetheless overcome by motivated reasoning disadvantages; present private evidence as simply part of what is privileged without weighing advantages *(outline 0 explicitly weighs and argues the advantage is overcome (section 6); outline 5 discusses evidence requiring interpretation (section 7); others present without explicit weighing)*

**Sharpest disagreement.** Whether to establish first-person privilege early (sections 2-3) before arguing for third-person superiority, or to establish third-person superiority first and then restrict first-person privilege later (sections 5-6). This determines whether the paper reads as restricting a presumed privilege or as discovering an unexpected superiority. The early-privilege approach (outlines 1, 3, 4) frames the argument as limiting traditional first-person authority; the late-privilege approach (outlines 0, 2, 5) frames it as establishing third-person superiority and only then clarifying what remains first-personally accessible. This changes whether the reader experiences the thesis as a restriction or a revelation.

**Absent from all of them.** None of the outlines addresses potential objections about cases where self-interpretation might be superior (e.g., nascent attitudes, attitudes about private experiences, or attitudes that are partly constituted by self-interpretation), nor do they discuss the practical or normative implications of the inverted asymmetry for practices of testimony, confession, or self-governance.
