# Duplicate groups among the leaders

## Group 1

*Shared claim:* The debate wrongly takes the individual sampled token or output as the unit of speech-act evaluation; assertion, if present, attaches to the model's output distribution or policy (what it reliably would say), which is also where the commitment objection must be assessed.

- index 1 · promise 5 · wounded — If any chatbot output is an assertion, the asserted content is not what a sampled token sequence says but what the model's output distribution commits to. The token-level framing used by every side is mistaken, and the 'no stable commitment' objection to chatbot assertion misfires.
- index 16 · promise 4 · wounded — The assertion debate is individuated at the wrong grain. Speech acts are token acts, but a chatbot's reply is a draw from a policy. The live question is whether assertion can attach to the policy (what the model reliably would say) rather than to the sampled token, and on that question the cases for denial and for proto-assertion come apart from where they are now placed.

## Group 2

*Shared claim:* The disagreement over whether 'the LLM' performs speech acts is really about which entity (token sample, policy or weights, persona, deployed system or vendor) counts as the speaker. 'The LLM' is not one determinate candidate, so the camps' verdicts differ because they refer to different things, not because they disagree about speech-act conditions.

- index 0 · promise 4 · dead — The disagreement among denial, proxy, fictionalist, and attributing views is not about the conditions for performing a speech act. It is about which entity in the LLM stack counts as the speaker (token-level sample, trained policy, deployed persona, or sociotechnical system). Once the speaker is fixed, the rival camps largely agree on the verdicts.
- index 35 · promise 4 · unattacked — Much of the disagreement over whether 'the LLM' performs speech acts is verbal, because 'the LLM' picks out different candidate speakers in different camps (weights, a running inference process, a persona, a deployed product, the vendor), and each camp's verdict is correct for its own referent.

## Group 3

*Shared claim:* Barandiaran and Almendros's category of 'purpose-structured but not purposeful' systems already supplies what illocutionary force requires, namely the act type's illocutionary point and not a speaker's purpose or autonomy. The intention and autonomy debate therefore targets the wrong determinant of speech-act performance.

- index 27 · promise 4 · unattacked — Barandiaran and Almendros's middle category, 'purpose-structured but not purposeful', runs together the illocutionary point of an act type and the purpose of a speaker. Speech-act performance requires only the former, so their own characterisation of LLMs is a description of the kind of system that can perform speech acts of its own.
- index 30 · promise 4 · unattacked — Barandiaran and Almendros's own category of tasks that are 'purpose-structured and purpose-bounded' but not purposeful gives illocutionary force everything it needs, so the whole intention-and-autonomy debate is aimed at the wrong determinant of speech-act identity.
