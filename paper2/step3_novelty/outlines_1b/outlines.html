Thesis (fixed)
If chatbot outputs are assertions, then hallucination, sycophancy and sanction are best assessed at the level of the trained policy (the output distribution and its calibration), not the sampled string, and resampling inconsistency is not evidence of insincerity. Whether the asserted content is the policy's calibrated 'p, with confidence c' is not established.

Nothing here is ranked and nothing is scored. The outlines were written blind to each other, and the flags below each one are computed from its own dependency ledger — no model judged coherence. Read the outlines first; the divergence section at the end is only useful once you have.

Outline 0 — The Unit of Assertion Failure: Assessing Chatbot Hallucination, Sycophancy and Sanction at the Level of the Policy

Grant the antecedent that chatbot outputs are assertions and ask which object bears assertion's defects; use a same-string/different-policy test to show the policy does (about a third of the words go to this load-bearing step), extend it to sanction and then to sincerity, and finish by showing these verdicts survive every content reading the evidence leaves open, including the unsettled 'p, with confidence c' reading.

Surprise
A specialist would expect the policy-level move to be argued from the metaphysics of the speaker (against Chalmers's thread or instance view). Here it is argued from a same-string/different-policy test that makes the choice of speaker nearly irrelevant, and the ML results (Kalai on grading, Sharma on preference training) are used as premises about what sanction is, so that string-level grading is shown to cause hallucination. A second surprise is that sycophancy, not resampling, is the evidence that would show insincerity.
Commitment
The outline commits to the string not being the unit of assessment even granting that every output is an assertion. It commits to sanction being a policy-level matter that deployer responsibility cannot replace, and to resampling disagreement having a likelihood ratio of about one for insincerity. It also commits to the open content question being a robustness test of these verdicts, not a hedge on them, so a cautious version that conditioned everything on calibrated 'p, with confidence c' would be the weaker paper.
Gap
§3, abductive — §3 moves from a causal fact (sanction works only by reshaping the output distribution) to a normative claim (the policy is the party sanctioned, and the sanction's adequacy is assessable there). This goes through only if the antecedent 'outputs are assertions' is read with a sanction concept on which credibility-tracking feedback onto the asserter's dispositions suffices, or on which the training objective gives the policy something at stake. Butlin & Viebahn's third element, that losing credibility be bad for the asserter, must then be met at policy level or be dispensable. If it requires interests that no policy has, the antecedent plus their condition is unsatisfiable and §3's claim is vacuous. A further requirement is that deployer responsibility is not an alternative locus for the same assessment, but a separate question about deployment decisions.

Outline 5 — The Policy Is Where the Assertion Goes Wrong: Hallucination, Sycophancy, Sanction and the Resampling Fallacy

Suppose chatbot outputs are assertions. Individuate the faults of an asserter (hallucination, sycophancy) by what they are, which shows they are distributional. Fix the level (trained policy, not string, thread, role or deployer) by showing that each rival level either inherits the fault from the policy or cannot register it. Then show that the content-and-calibration question stays open, and that this openness is what strips resampling inconsistency of its force as evidence of insincerity. Finally show that the level verdicts survive every live answer to the content question.

Surprise
A specialist would expect the content question ('p, with confidence c') to appear as a closing concession. Here it is argued early as a premise (§5) and does the work of disarming the resampling-as-insincerity inference. A specialist would also expect Chalmers's incoherence argument against the model-as-speaker to be a threat to a policy-level view. Here cross-context contradiction becomes the very data of sycophancy assessment, and the consistent systematic sycophant is used to show that resampling consistency clears no one.
Commitment
The outline commits to the claim that sycophancy cannot be assessed at the level of the string or the thread at all, not merely that the policy level is also available. It commits to the claim that deployer sanctionability is no substitute for sanction of the asserter, and that the level verdicts hold on every live answer to the content question. A cautious version would say policy-level assessment is 'a useful complement' and would not stake the verdict on the content hypothesis.
Gap
