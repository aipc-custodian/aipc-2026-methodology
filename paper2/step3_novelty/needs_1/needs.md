# Works to be read in full

**Thesis:** If chatbot outputs are assertions, hallucination, sycophancy and sanction are best assessed at the level of the trained policy, and resampling inconsistency is not evidence of insincerity. Whether the asserted content is the policy's calibrated 'p, with confidence c' is not established.

Fetch these and put the files in one folder. Add nothing the list does not ask for.

- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Butlin, Patrick & Viebahn, Emanuel (2025). 'AI Assertion'. Ergo: An Open Access Journal of Philosophy 12, pp. 968–988. doi:10.3998/ergo.7960  
  The paper needs their descriptive-function and sanctionability conditions, and their verdict that fine-tuned models fail the sanction condition. The thesis targets how they treat consistency and credibility-tracking, and the full text must be checked for whether retraining or any policy-level bearer is considered.  
  https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/ergo/article/id/7960/print/  (open access)
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Williams, Iwan & Bayne, Tim (2026). 'Chatting with Bots: AI, Speech-Acts, and the Edge of Assertion'. Inquiry 69(6): 3059–3082 (arXiv:2410.16645, 2024). doi:10.1080/0020174X.2024.2434874  
  This is the primary target. The paper needs their objection from commitment and consistency, their proto-assertion category, and their rejection of proxy-assertion and fictionalism. The full text will settle whether any policy-level bearer or resampling point is considered.  
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16645  (open access)
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Shanahan, Murray, McDonell, Kyle & Reynolds, Laria (2023). 'Role play with large language models'. Nature 623, pp. 493–498.  
  It is the framework closest to treating an LLM agent as a distribution (a 'superposition of simulacra') rather than a single output. The paper must show how its policy-level unit differs from this and from the role-play verdict.  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06647-8
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Levinstein, Benjamin A. & Herrmann, Daniel A. (2025). 'Still no lie detector for language models: probing empirical and conceptual roadblocks'. Philosophical Studies 182(7): 1539–1565. doi:10.1007/s11098-023-02094-3  
  A referee will expect the paper to have absorbed their conceptual and empirical treatment of whether and how LLM beliefs can be measured. The paper also needs it for the lying/belief connection and for the limits on reading a model's belief state off its outputs.  
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00175  (open access)
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Herrmann, Daniel A. & Levinstein, Benjamin A. (2025). 'Standards for Belief Representations in LLMs'. Minds and Machines 35(1). doi:10.1007/s11023-024-09709-6  
  It sets out criteria for when LLM internal states count as beliefs. I have not read the full text, but it looks like the nearest source for what must hold of a policy-level 'p with confidence c' before it can be called calibrated belief.  
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11023-024-09709-6
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Mallory, Fintan (2023). 'Fictionalism about Chatbots'. Ergo: An Open Access Journal of Philosophy 10, article 38, pp. 1082–1100. doi:10.3998/ergo.4668  
  It is the main rival that denies assertion (prop-oriented make-believe). It is also the fictionalist position that Williams and Bayne reject, so the paper must say why the policy-level reading is not fictionalism.  
  https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/ergo/article/id/4668/print/  (open access)
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Lin, Stephanie C., Hilton, Jacob & Evans, Owain (2022). 'Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words'. Transactions on Machine Learning Research. arXiv:2205.14334  
  It is the primary source for 'verbalized probability', that is, a model producing an answer together with a calibrated confidence. That is the empirical template for the thesis's 'p, with confidence c'.  
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14334  (open access)
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Kadavath, Saurav, et al. (2022). 'Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know'. arXiv:2207.05221 (Anthropic)  
  It supplies the evidence that model confidence (P(True), P(IK)) is calibrated at the model level, and it uses resampled outputs. It bears directly on whether the policy's content is a calibrated 'p with confidence c' and on treating hallucination as miscalibration.  
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.05221  (open access)
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Manakul, Potsawee, Liusie, Adian & Gales, Mark J. F. (2023). 'SelfCheckGPT: Zero-Resource Black-Box Hallucination Detection for Generative Large Language Models'. Proceedings of EMNLP 2023, pp. 9004–9017. doi:10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.557  
  It is the primary source for resampling inconsistency as a signal of hallucination (an epistemic-state signal), not of insincerity. The paper must handle the empirical link between spread across samples and falsehood.  
  https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.557/  (open access)
- [ ] **ESSENTIAL** — Sharma, Mrinank, Tong, Meg, Korbak, Tomasz, et al. (2024). 'Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models'. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.13548  
  It is the standard empirical treatment of sycophancy as a systematic effect of RLHF training and preference data. This supports assessing sycophancy at the level of the trained policy and not of single outputs.  
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13548  (open access)
- [ ] **USEFUL** — Skiba, Lukas (2026). 'Artificial Speech Acts'. Analysis, anag022. doi:10.1093/analys/anag022  
  It generalizes the norm-based argument against LLM assertion to all speech acts. A referee would expect the paper's conditional ('if outputs are assertions') to be tested against this argument.  
  https://dx.doi.org/10.1093/analys/anag022
- [ ] **USEFUL** — Massaguer Gómez, Germán (2026). 'Assertion, Accountability, and Large Language Models'. Philosophy & Technology 39, article 123.  
  It is a rival account of where sanction and accountability land when LLM outputs function as assertions (assertion without asserter). The thesis must distinguish its policy-level sanction from this human-and-institutional account.  
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13347-026-01136-y  (open access)
- [ ] **USEFUL** — Wang, Zhou, et al. (2026). 'Are LLM Decisions Faithful to Verbal Confidence?'. arXiv:2601.07767  
  It reports that verbal confidence can be calibrated while behaviour (abstention) does not follow it. This is direct evidence against assuming a single policy-level 'p with confidence c', which is the thesis's own open caveat.  
  https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07767v1  (open access)
- [ ] **USEFUL** — Ganapini, Marianna Bergamaschi, Sokol, Anna, et al. (2026). 'Do LLMs have core beliefs?'. arXiv:2605.03255  
  It is empirical pressure on whether a stable, policy-level commitment profile exists. Only the first two authors are confirmed, and the full author list needs checking when the paper is read.  
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.03255  (open access)
