Thesis (fixed)
If chatbot outputs are assertions, then hallucination, sycophancy and sanction are best assessed at the level of the trained policy (the output distribution and its calibration), not the sampled string, and resampling inconsistency is not evidence of insincerity. Whether the asserted content is the policy's calibrated 'p, with confidence c' is not established.

Nothing here is ranked and nothing is scored. The outlines were written blind to each other, and the flags below each one are computed from its own dependency ledger — no model judged coherence. Read the outlines first; the divergence section at the end is only useful once you have.

Outline 0 — Judge the Policy, Not the String: Assertion, Hallucination, Sycophancy and Resampling in Chatbots

Supposing chatbot outputs are assertions, show that the three failures named in the thesis are constituted at the level of the output distribution; that this holds whoever the speaker is; that what the policy asserts is left open; that resampling divergence then fails to indicate insincerity; and that the policy beats the string and the deployer as the unit of assessment.

Surprise
The structure separates the locus of assessment from the identity of the speaker. This lets the paper grant Chalmers's case against the model-as-speaker in full and still reach a policy-level verdict. It also makes the open-content claim a constraint that §5's argument has to survive on every candidate content, not a closing caveat.
Commitment
The paper commits to inconsistency being no evidence of insincerity, not weak evidence, and to the string being the wrong unit for these three assessments even while conceding that per-assertion norm violation is string-level. It refuses to rest on the credence reading of content, leaving the content claim open at the thesis's own strength. It also stakes the sanction claim against Gómez and Butlin & Viebahn on the ground that deployer assessment is downstream of the policy.
Gap
§5, abductive — From 'sample divergence is fixed by the distribution and the decoding setting' (with §4 leaving the content open) the paper infers 'divergence is not evidence of insincerity'. This goes through only if, for every live candidate content, sincerity and insincerity predict the same divergence once the policy's distribution is given. The candidates are bare p at the mode and p-with-c. It also requires that sampled departures from the mode, on the bare-p reading, be classed as overassertion (a calibration fault already assessed under hallucination) and not as assertions the policy does not believe. That in turn needs a notion of the policy's attitude on which a wide distribution is not itself disbelief in the sampled string, and no independent probe of that attitude (Levinstein & Herrmann) is available to overturn it. If a threshold view of belief is right, on which asserting a non-modal sample is asserting what one disbelieves, the step fails. This is the point of greatest distance between what §§2-4 establish and what is claimed.

5Divergence among samples drawn from one prompt is fixed by the policy's output distribution and the decoding setting, so it is evidence of how wide the distribution is and not evidence of insincerity.
Supplies the thesis's second conjunct. §6 needs it, and it is where the paper meets its named target.		A showing that, on some live candidate content for the policy's assertions, samples that depart from the policy's mode count as assertions mismatched with the policy's attitude in a way that is not just the miscalibration or overassertion already assessed under hallucination. The argument has four parts. (i) Temperature can be set to zero with the policy unchanged, so variance indexes the decoding setting and distribution width, not an attitude. (ii) Insincerity is a mismatch between what is asserted and the asserter's attitude, and resampling supplies no independent evidence of the attitude; Levinstein & Herrmann show the independent route (probes) fails. (iii) On a thread-style individuation, sibling branches are different speakers, so divergence between them is not one speaker contradicting itself. (iv) On the bare-p reading, wide-distribution overassertion is a fault of warrant (calibration), a different axis from insincerity.
