# The drafter's response to the rulings

**MUST ADDRESS concerns**

1. **Bearer vs. ground.** I split (L) into a negative part, a grounding part and a bearer part (§2.1), and I now say outright that the test only establishes the first two. For the bearer part there is a new argument. A human assertion bears the defect because there is a gap between act and state: someone with impeccable evidence can still assert beyond it. A policy has no such gap, because decoding (the act-disposition) and the credal profile (the state) are both parts of the policy. So "asserting beyond confidence" is a mismatch inside the policy, and the string has the property only derivatively. For sycophancy the point is stronger, since it is a counterfactual property. I also concede that strings may still be called hallucinatory in a derivative sense. §2.5 now treats the thread's assertion as derivatively defective, so the Chalmers discussion no longer gives the point away.

2. **Mass ≠ warrant; mass-matching vs. calibration; greedy and collapsed policies.** I concede that matching force to mass is not warrant. §2.2 now defines warrant by c\*, a calibration-corrected confidence: the frequency of truth among claims the policy gives similar confidence, within a reference class. It is a property of the weights and does not depend on decoding. With this criterion the RLHF-collapsed variant of P₃ hallucinates, and greedy decoding does not make P₄ warranted. P₃ counts as warranted only because its 0.95-claims are reliable, not because of corpus frequency. I dropped the internalist phrase "the asserter's own information". I label the account reliabilist, credit Goldman (1979), and acknowledge the generality problem.

3. **Provenance.** §2.4 now has a provenance subsection. I choose the behavioural-reliabilist account and say so. I argue that differences in provenance usually show up as differences in conditional distributions across counterfactual contexts. Where no context separates two systems, I accept the externalist bullet. I also explain that Case 2's mention of sources describes the cause of the distribution, not the ground of the verdict.

4. **R3 reverses the Case 1–2 verdicts.** I concede this in §5.3 and correct the abstract and the conclusion. I now spell out what survives on R3: the sycophancy verdict, §4, and the locus of assessment. On the last point I argue that whether R3 holds is itself a question about the policy. The dangling "clause (a)/(b)" references are gone.

5. **Corrective intervention vs. sanction; the thermometer.** §3.1 separates the subject of sanction, the object of intervention and the criterion of success. I withdraw the "thin analogue of (iii)" reply (§3.3), conceding that it applies to any self-calibrating instrument, and I quote Butlin and Viebahn's thermometer passage. The sanction claim is now explicitly conditional: on theories that admit chatbot assertion with a weaker sanction component, the lineage meets it, and those theories must exclude thermometers on other grounds. Sambrotta is credited for this point. The professor analogy is now used to support the two-level structure.

6. **Lineage and the frozen policy.** §3.2 defines a policy lineage, admits that its boundaries are vague (distillation, merging), and concedes that a frozen πₜ cannot be sensitive to anything. Verdicts attach to πₜ (synchronic) and sensitivity to the lineage (diachronic). The professor/dispositions analogy shows how the two levels fit together.

7. **"String-level sanction is self-defeating."** §3.4 narrows the claim. Only regimes that score responses for truth or approval *alone* defeat themselves. I concede that Kalai et al.'s remedy is applied response by response, and argue that its rationale is nonetheless policy-level. The Nature version is now cited.

8. **Likelihood ratio.** I drop the claim of a ratio near one. §4.2 now gives P(E) = 1 − Σrᵢᵏ, argues that E is evidence about spread only, and argues that selective decoupling in a fixed context tends to concentrate mass. The conclusion is now "E does not support insincerity", and I state that the strength of evidence from consistency depends on priors I do not fix.
