# The drafter's response to the rulings

**MUST ADDRESS 1: getting from grounding to bearer (§2.1 "no gap" argument).** I dropped the "no gap between act and state" argument. In its place is a new §2.5 built on a stated principle, *Difference-making*. A token act bears a defect non-derivatively only if something in the producer at the time of the act separates it from a non-defective act the producer could have issued in the same situation. On each of the three weak points the referees found:
- *Deterministic humans.* The principle holds under determinism. What makes the difference for a human is her deliberative state at the time of acting; no libertarian choice is needed.
- *Standing dispositions.* I concede that a person with a standing disposition to overassert has a trait-level defect. Humans have both levels; chatbots have only the trait level, because two draws from one policy in one context differ only by the random seed. Under greedy decoding every fact about the draw is a fact about the policy.
- *The decoding point.* The q/π gap is described as a mismatch inside one object, comparable to the trait.

I also concede that strings bear falsity, causal responsibility for harm and the derivative label. And I state a fallback: if Difference-making is rejected, (L) shrinks to its grounding part plus the claim that the policy is what must be evaluated and changed, and §§3–4 need only that weaker reading. Following the suggestion to separate what explains, what grounds and what bears, §2.1 now says outright that the test establishes only the first two parts of (L).

**MUST ADDRESS 2: the scale for force f.** §2.2 now defines force as the least calibrated confidence at which uttering s, with its hedging, is licensed in context x. A flat assertion has a contextual threshold τₓ below 1, and hedges lower it. The cases need only that τₓ lies between 0.5 and 0.95. That range makes P₁ (c\* = 0.95) warranted, and P₂ (c\* = 0.01) and the collapsed policy (c\* = 0.5) unwarranted.

**MUST ADDRESS 3: whether the lineage is the only system-level candidate.** §3.1's account of the "object" now includes persistent-state updates (memory, retrieval, system prompts revised by deployers). §3.2 widens the lineage to a sequence of *effective policies* π_θₜ(· | Mₜ, u), changed by updates to the weights, the state, or both. Threads become short lineages. Sociotechnical systems meet the component either through their human members (the deployer option) or through a lineage. §3.3 adds that a theory which drops the sanction condition altogether gives the conditional no application. The abstract and conclusion were changed to match.

**MUST ADDRESS 4: what resampling disagreement can show.** I withdrew the categorical claim and the claim that insincerity "if anything" predicts less spread. §4.2 now argues three things. The spread profile screens off sincerity from disagreement. The direction of any link depends on priors, and the referee's toy case (likelihood ratio about 2.8) is given as a real possibility. Every insincere policy has a *sincere twin* with the same within-context spread, so disagreement cannot tell them apart and diagnostic tests must vary the audience cue. §4.1 now measures decoupling against a neutral context x₀, which also takes up the point that one set of weights fixes both q and π. I acknowledge that the result leans on the definition of insincerity and point to §4.3 as where that definition is defended.
